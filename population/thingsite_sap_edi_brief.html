03The physical bridge SAP doesn't have — EPCIS

SAP posts a goods issue and a goods receipt. It does not, natively, reconcile those postings against the physical scan at the dock. Our EPCIS 2.0 engine does — the SSCC on the DESADV becomes an EPCIS shipping event; the scan at receiving becomes a receiving event; the ThingSite reconciles both against the SAP goods movement. Discrepancy surfaces as a discrepancy, not a silent mismatch.

The engine enforces the GS1 CBV as a closed vocabulary (unknown term throws), fails closed (a rejected post leaves the store structurally unchanged), and never defaults an event time — a defaulted timestamp in a custody chain is a guess. Registrar discipline, in the transaction layer.

SAP goods issue↔ EPCIS shipping event (SSCC)…dock scan… EPCIS receiving event↔ SAP goods receipt

04The loop closes at the pallet

The SSCC on the DESADV/856 is the smart pallet. In the EDI/SAP trading-partner agreement, the platform attribute — wood, block, plastic — is a rule that fires a repalletization. Retailer mandates (Sam's Club's 2025 block mandate, Costco) make it a ratchet. Each pallet is a GRAI; CHEP alone pools ~300 million. EDI, SAP, EPCIS, and the returnable asset are one chain — and it runs through the ThingSite.

06Worked example — GA and Diazyme

A concrete slice of GA's own supply chain: GA runs the 270 protocol; Diazyme supplies the consumables it runs on. A GA→Diazyme purchase order (ORDERS/850) carries Diazyme's PGLN, GA's receiving-plant GLN, and the consumable GTINs. The DESADV/856 back carries the SSCC pallet and batch LGTINs. On the ThingSite, those resolve to one graph: GA's instrument (GIAI) — runs the 270 protocol (GDTI) — consumes Diazyme's SGTINs — supplied by Diazyme (PGLN) — received at GA's lab (GLN). The same purchase order SAP posts as a transaction, the ThingSite holds as a relationship — and the EPCIS scan at GA's dock reconciles it against the goods receipt.
